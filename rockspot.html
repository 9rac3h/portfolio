Academic Website Redesign

Rock Spot Climbing

Refine the information architecture and pages to create a clearer course selection and purchasing path for users.

Role

UX/UI Designer

Timeline

Jan 2026 – Apr 2026

Methods

Brand Review, Content Audit, Competitive Review, Wireframing

Focus

Program discovery, booking flow, e-commerce experience

Project Overview

A climbing gym website redesign

Rock Spot Climbing is a regional gym network offering open climbing, instruction, youth programs, memberships, and supporting resources. Its website needed to serve people arriving with very different levels of confidence and prior knowledge.

I redesigned two connected journeys: helping visitors find an age- and skill-appropriate program and move toward availability, while creating a more complete shopping path from product discovery to purchase consideration.

Research & Key Insights

Finding a clearer path into climbing

I reviewed Rock Spot's brand, audiences, content, and comparable gym experiences to understand where the website could offer more guidance without losing the depth needed by returning climbers.

01

Brand Direction

Rock Spot's strongest qualities are accessibility, safety, community, and personal growth. The digital experience needed to feel as welcoming and supportive as the gym itself, rather than relying on intimidating or elite-focused climbing language.

02

Priority Audiences

I identified three primary audience contexts: first-time climbers seeking reassurance, returning climbers looking for training and memberships, and parents comparing structured youth programs.

03

Content & Navigation Audit

Classes, memberships, locations, safety information, and policies were all useful, but their separation across deep navigation made it harder to understand what applied to a user's age, experience, or immediate goal.

04

Design Direction

The research pointed toward a progressive journey: lower the barrier to a first visit, organize programs by age and skill level, and connect program understanding directly to location and availability.

This academic redesign was based on brand discovery, desk research, content review, and iterative wireframing; it did not include interviews or usability sessions with Rock Spot customers.

Two Experience Gaps

Two journeys needed clearer next steps

The redesign focused on the two tasks where clearer structure could make the biggest difference: choosing a program and shopping for climbing products.

01

Program discovery & booking

Visitors needed to connect age, ability level, location, and availability before they could judge whether a class was appropriate.

02

Online shopping

The store needed a connected path for browsing categories, filtering products, reviewing details, and moving toward a purchase.

Wireframe Exploration

Mapping the core journeys before visual design

I used wireframes to test hierarchy across the full system rather than refining one page in isolation. The explorations covered discovery, comparison, detail, and action states for both programs and shopping.

Design Decision 01

From program discovery to availability

The program flow progressively answers three questions: which program fits, which level is appropriate, and where it is available. Users can compare options before committing, then move from program understanding toward a nearby class.

03

Continue to a nearby class

Location, postcode, and date selection are placed directly after the level decision.

Design Decision 02

Building a more complete shopping journey

The store experience connects category discovery with practical comparison. Product filters reduce scanning effort, while the detail page brings variants, fulfillment, reviews, and related products into one purchase decision.

Supporting Experience

A welcoming entry point for first-time climbers

The homepage supports the two primary journeys with direct first-visit guidance, a nearby-location search, visible program categories, and community reassurance. It acts as an entry point rather than the case study's main story.

01

First-visit support

Gear, guidance, safety, and community are surfaced early.

02

Location-first action

The search module connects interest with a nearby gym.

03

Connected ecosystem

Programs, memberships, events, and shopping share one visual system.

Outcome

From scattered information to a beginner-friendly journey

This project strengthened my ability to use wireframes and information architecture across multiple connected journeys. The final direction gives program discovery a clearer route toward availability and extends the same system into product browsing and evaluation, while keeping the overall experience welcoming to first-time climbers.
