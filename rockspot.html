Academic Website Redesign

Rock Spot Climbing

Redesigning a climbing gym website into a clearer, beginner-friendly experience for first visits, program discovery, and class booking.

Role

UX/UI Designer

Timeline

Jan 2026 – Apr 2026

Methods

Brand Review, Content Audit, Competitive Review, Wireframing

Focus

Information architecture, first-time flow, program browsing

Project Overview

Creating a clearer starting point for new climbers

Rock Spot Climbing is a regional gym network offering open climbing, instruction, youth programs, memberships, and supporting resources. Its website needed to serve people arriving with very different levels of confidence and prior knowledge.

I redesigned the experience around clearer user paths: prepare for a first visit, find an age- and skill-appropriate program, understand what to expect, and move toward a nearby class or membership.

Research & Key Insights

Finding a clearer path into climbing

I reviewed Rock Spot's brand, audiences, content, and comparable gym experiences to understand where the website could offer more guidance without losing the depth needed by returning climbers.

01

Brand Direction

Rock Spot's strongest qualities are accessibility, safety, community, and personal growth. The digital experience needed to feel as welcoming and supportive as the gym itself, rather than relying on intimidating or elite-focused climbing language.

02

Priority Audiences

I identified three primary audience contexts: first-time climbers seeking reassurance, returning climbers looking for training and memberships, and parents comparing structured youth programs.

03

Content & Navigation Audit

Classes, memberships, locations, safety information, and policies were all useful, but their separation across deep navigation made it harder to understand what applied to a user's age, experience, or immediate goal.

04

Design Direction

The research pointed toward a progressive journey: lower the barrier to a first visit, organize programs by age and skill level, and connect program understanding directly to location and availability.

This academic redesign was based on brand discovery, desk research, content review, and iterative wireframing; it did not include interviews or usability sessions with Rock Spot customers.

Key Problems

A welcoming brand hidden behind a complex path

First-time visitors needed reassurance about safety, gear, and what would happen during a visit, while returning climbers wanted fast access to locations, memberships, and advanced programs. Treating these needs as one undifferentiated navigation structure made the site feel heavier than the brand itself.

Program information also required users to connect age, experience level, location, and availability across separate pages. For parents and new climbers, that increased the effort required to decide whether a class was appropriate before taking action.

Wireframe

Mapping the homepage as a guided onboarding flow

I started with wireframes to test the order of information before refining visual style. The homepage wireframe organized the experience around first-time support, program categories, membership value, events, community proof, and a newsletter/contact section.

Early homepage wireframe mapping the experience from first-time support to programs, events, community, and newsletter sign-up.

Design Decision 01

Making the first visit feel less intimidating

The homepage introduces Rock Spot with direct language, a location search, and a first-time support section. Instead of assuming prior knowledge, the design surfaces gear, guidance, safety, and community as part of the first-visit path.

This translates the brand's inclusive positioning into concrete answers for users who are interested in climbing but unsure how to begin.

Design Decision 02

Helping users find the right class faster

For the programs page, I organized discovery around the factors that determine fit: audience, age, location, and course level. This lets users compare relevant options without reading through a long undifferentiated list.

Supporting media makes the class environment more tangible and reduces uncertainty before a user commits.

Design Decision 03

Turning program details into an availability path

The Senders Program detail page breaks the decision into clearer sections: overview, learning goals, intended audience, level options, and class availability. This helps parents and new climbers determine fit before choosing a level or searching for a nearby class.

The flow connects information with action, moving users from program understanding to location and date selection.

Final Design

A more structured website for climbing discovery

The final design system uses stronger visual hierarchy, clearer calls to action, and more consistent page patterns across the homepage, program browsing, and program detail pages.

This structure helps users move from curiosity to action: learning what to expect, finding the right program, and checking availability at a nearby location.

Outcome

From scattered information to a beginner-friendly journey

This project strengthened my ability to use wireframes and information architecture to shape a website redesign. The final experience is more welcoming for first-time climbers and more structured for users comparing programs, memberships, locations, and class options.
