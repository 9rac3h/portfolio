Six-Week Team Project · UX Research · Interaction Design

Cluzy Event Check-In

Designing for limited attention at live events.

A reward-led mobile flow that makes a short data exchange feel fast, clear, and worth the effort.

Role

UX/UI Designer

Timeline

6 weeks · Fall 2025

Team

Team of 3

Focus

Research, check-in, reward & conversion

The Challenge

How might we help event attendees complete a data check-in quickly enough that the reward still feels worth the effort?

Research

Three interviews pointed to one rule: make the value obvious and keep the effort low.

We ran three 30-minute remote interviews with prototype walkthroughs, focusing on check-in speed, reward motivation, mood input, and privacy.

01

Speed was already a strength

Participants consistently described the prototype as fast, simple, and easy to complete; one finished in about 30 seconds.

02

The reward has to feel worth it

Reward value and visibility affected willingness to scan. Participants wanted to know what they would receive upfront.

03

Clarity matters more than novelty

“Region” caused confusion, while emoji-only mood input felt ambiguous to some participants.

04

Trust depends on restraint

Users preferred minimal data collection and judged legitimacy largely through the event organizer and context.

Key Decision 01

Show the value before asking for effort.

The final landing page makes the $5 reward, expected time, privacy promise, and instant redemption scannable before the user commits to the form.

Why: reward transparency was one of the clearest drivers of participation in interviews.

→

Key Decision 02

Cut the form from 10 questions to 6.

The early check-in collected a broad set of demographic and behavioral data. I reduced the final flow to six questions and made identity optional so the interaction stayed closer to the speed users already valued.

10questions→6questions

Why: in a live event environment, every extra field competes with the user’s immediate goal.

→

Key Decision 03 · User Goal vs Business Goal

Deliver the reward first. Ask for registration second.

The attendee scanned for a reward; Cluzy wanted continued engagement. Instead of merging both goals, I separated them into a clear sequence.

User goal

Claim the promised reward

Complete the check-in and use the voucher immediately.

Business goal

Create a reason to continue

Explain Cluzy’s ongoing value and invite registration without blocking redemption.

→

Trust Without Friction

Privacy cues stay visible without turning the flow into legal copy.

Research suggested that users cared about minimal information and legitimacy more than reading a long privacy policy. Short trust cues stay near the moments where data is requested.

30 Secondssets an effort expectation
100% Privatemakes the data promise visible
Optional identityreduces unnecessary disclosure
Maybe Laterkeeps registration voluntary

End-to-End Experience

Value → check-in → reward → optional conversion

Each screen has one primary job, keeping the physical-event interaction short while still supporting Cluzy’s longer-term business goal.

See the reward→Answer 6 questions→Claim voucher→See Cluzy value→Register or leave

What Testing Changed

Research helped me decide what to simplify — not just what to restyle.

Interview feedback reinforced speed as a strength while exposing ambiguity around reward details, data collection, and some input methods. The final iteration focused on a smaller form, clearer incentive, and cleaner task separation.

Next Validation

  • Completion timeCan users still finish comfortably in under a minute?
  • Reward comprehensionDo users understand the value before they start?
  • Drop-offWhere do attendees abandon the six-question flow?
  • Optional conversionCan users distinguish voucher redemption from account registration?

Reflection

Efficiency is about task order, not just fewer screens.

Cluzy taught me to balance immediate user value with a business conversion goal. The strongest improvement was not adding more guidance — it was deciding what the user needed to see now, and what could wait until after the reward.
