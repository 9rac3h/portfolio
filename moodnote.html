Independent Product Design · Emotional Wellness

MoodNote

Designing emotional wellness without adding emotional pressure.

A mobile experience for reflection, gentle reset, and private or trusted sharing — designed for moments when users may have limited emotional bandwidth.

Role

Product & UI Designer

Timeline

Jul–Aug 2026

Tools

Figma, Prototyping

Focus

Interaction, UX Writing, End-to-End Flow

The Challenge

Wellness shouldn’t feel like another task.

People may open a wellness app when they are already stressed, tired, or overwhelmed. In those moments, long forms, too many choices, or achievement-focused language can add friction instead of support.

MoodNote explores how a product can help users reflect and take a small next step while asking for as little emotional effort as possible.

How Might We

How might we design an emotional wellness experience that feels supportive without becoming another source of pressure?

Starting Point

Designing for limited emotional bandwidth

As a concept project without primary user research, I treated these as design assumptions to test — not research findings.

01

Starting can feel like work

Reflection should be easy to begin without requiring a full journal entry, photo, or lengthy setup.

02

Too many choices create pressure

Multiple equally weighted actions can increase decision effort when users are already overwhelmed.

03

Sharing needs control

Wanting support does not mean wanting to share everything. Privacy should stay explicit and reversible.

04

Language carries emotional weight

Prompts and CTAs should invite action without judgment, urgency, or a “correct” way to feel.

Design Principles

Three rules guided the experience

01

Reduce the effort

Keep required inputs light, use optional content, and offer manageable session lengths.

02

Preserve control

Let users choose privacy, pace, duration, and whether to skip, pause, or stop.

03

Invite, don’t instruct

Use neutral, non-judgmental language that supports without pushing users toward a behavior.

Structure

Reflection and support needed to connect without becoming one rigid path

I mapped the product around distinct spaces for reflection, recharge, sharing, and review, then connected them through lightweight transitions.

Key Decision 01

Make reflection easy to start

Photo upload became optional so the core task stays emotional reflection, not content creation. Mood tags offer a lower-effort alternative when writing feels like too much.

Design goal: reduce interaction cost without removing expressive options.

Key Decision 02

Reduce choice pressure, not choice

The earlier Recharge screen gave three activities nearly equal visual weight. I revised the hierarchy around one recommended option while keeping alternatives visible.

Design goal: lower decision effort while preserving user control.

→

Key Decision 03

Privacy is a choice, not a default

MoodNote separates private reflection from trusted sharing at the moment of creation. The selected privacy state then stays visible after saving and inside the moment detail.

Design goal: keep emotional sharing explicit, predictable, and user-controlled.

Key Decision 04 · UX Writing

Invite, don’t instruct.

I treated interface language as part of the interaction design. Labels and CTAs avoid urgency, judgment, or implying that users need to “fix” how they feel.

Pressure-prone wordingMoodNote approach
Complete your daily check-inHow are you feeling today?
Start your meditationChoose what you need right now.
You did it!You made space for yourself.
SkipSkip for now
Complete the sessionYou can pause or finish at any time.

End-to-End Experience

Reflection → action → understanding

The product does not stop at logging a mood. It connects reflection to a manageable action, then closes the loop with an optional check-in and weekly pattern review.

Reflect→Create Moment→Choose Privacy→Recharge→Session→Check-in→Weekly Review

How I’d Validate It

Success should mean less friction, not more activity.

The next step would be usability testing and measuring whether users can engage comfortably — not simply whether they spend more time in the app.

Reflection completion

Can users start and save a moment without unnecessary drop-off?

Decision ease

Can users choose a recharge option quickly and understand the recommendation?

Privacy confidence

Do users understand who can see a moment before and after saving?

Return value

Does Weekly Review help users recognize patterns worth returning for?

Reflection

Calm is not only a visual style.

This project shifted my focus from making calm-looking screens to designing calm interactions. Reducing pressure also meant considering pace, privacy, wording, and how much the product asks from a user in each moment.
